Results: which simple method reads rider messages best?

All 40 example messages were labelled by hand as negative, neutral or positive. Each method was scored against those labels.

MethodAccuracyMacro F1
Always guess the most common label42%0.20
TF-IDF + logistic regression (5-fold cross-validation)45%0.33
VADER lexicon (no training)80%0.78

Macro F1 averages the score for each label, so a method that ignores the neutral label is penalized.

Label mix

Negative and positive messages are about equal. Neutral is the smallest group.

Honest finding: with only 40 messages, a trained model cannot learn much. TF-IDF with logistic regression scored 45%, barely above always guessing "negative" (42%). The ready-made VADER lexicon scored 80% with no training. A trained model needs far more labelled messages before it can compete.

Where the lexicon gets it wrong

VADER scores words, not meaning. These are the messages where it disagreed with the human label.

Complaints without angry words are missed. "Felt very unsafe" and "blew past my stop" read as positive, because words like "clearly" and "security" score well.
Questions look neutral. "Why do buses bunch together?" is a complaint, but it has no negative word for the lexicon to find.

Limits and human oversight

Known limits

  • The 40 messages are short examples written for testing, not collected social media posts. They show how the methods behave, not what Toronto riders think.
  • One person labelled them, so there is no check on how often two people would agree.
  • Sarcasm, questions and polite complaints are hard for a lexicon. The error table shows real cases.
  • The code includes an off-the-shelf DistilBERT option and topic models. They were not scored here, because 40 messages are too few to judge them.

Before real use

  • Collect a few thousand real messages with permission and a clear privacy note.
  • Have two people label a sample and measure how often they agree.
  • Report results by topic, such as safety, so serious complaints are not lost in an average.
  • Keep a person reading flagged safety messages. A score should never be the only response to a safety report.

Reliable AI checklist

The six questions of the Reliable AI Adoption Framework, answered for this project.

Business problem

Help a transit team sort rider feedback by tone and topic, so complaints reach the right people faster.

Data readiness

40 example messages, hand-labelled. Enough to test the pipeline, not enough to train or to describe real riders.

Governance and risk

Real posts would contain usernames and locations. Any real use needs consent, data minimization and a privacy review.

Technology and tools

A lexicon (VADER) beat a trained model on this little data, so the simpler tool wins until more data exists.

People and adoption

Scores sort the queue. People read the messages, especially anything about safety.

Outcomes and monitoring

Re-score against fresh human labels every quarter, and track errors on safety messages separately.
